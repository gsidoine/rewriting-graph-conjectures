# Support-Cayley rigidity versus Kempe accessibility

**28 October 2026.** Human proofs and independently enumerated finite examples. **No Lean kernel verification; no resolution of an Erdős problem; no literature-priority claim.** The group-action argument, unique 3-edge-colorability and triangle expansions are classical ingredients.

## A general support-Cayley theorem

Suppose a finite group Gamma acts freely and transitively on a finite certificate space S contained in A^I, and the action preserves Hamming distance. Choose a reference certificate s0. Set

    w(g) = number of coordinates on which g*s0 differs from s0;
    T_r = {g ≠ identity : w(g) ≤ r};
    H_r = subgroup generated by T_r.

Under the **unrestricted feasible-to-feasible move rule** allowing any transition changing at most r coordinates, the reconfiguration graph is the Cayley graph Cay(Gamma,T_r). It has exactly [Gamma:H_r] connected components, each of size |H_r|.

**Proof.** Freeness and transitivity identify certificates with group elements. Isometry gives d(g*s0,h*s0) = w(g⁻¹h). Thus a transition occurs exactly when g⁻¹h ∈ T_r, and connected components are the left cosets g H_r. This proof is elementary; the underlying group-action result is not claimed novel.

This extends the view of old-component gauge torsors in [finite-group certificate repair](group_potential_repair_2026-10-28.md) but does **not** assume that the original constraints are group equations. The group action may appear only after solving a nonlinear certificate system.

## Sharp nonlinear example: cubic proper edge-colorings

Let G_k arise from K4 by k successive Y-to-triangle expansions (replace a cubic vertex by a triangle, attaching its three incident edges to the three new vertices). Then n=4+2k and m=3n/2. These are connected, simple, planar, 3-edge-connected cubic graphs.

**Classical rigidity lemma.** K4 has three perfect matchings which are its unique unlabelled 3-edge-coloring; there are six labelled colorings. A Y-to-triangle expansion preserves this count: the three external edge colors of the triangle must be pairwise different, by three-cut parity, and uniquely determine the three internal triangle edge colors. Contracting the triangle gives the inverse correspondence. Thus G_k has precisely six proper labeled 3-edge-colorings, one free transitive S3 orbit under global permutation of color names.

Every color class has n/2 edges. Therefore:
- a color transposition changes exactly n edge labels;
- a 3-cycle of color names changes all 3n/2 edge labels;
- changing the prescribed color of any single edge requires exactly n recolored edges, attained by the corresponding color transposition;
- for move budget r<n the six feasible states are isolated; for n≤r<3n/2 the move graph is K3,3; for r≥3n/2 it is K6;
- any union of two color classes is a *single Hamiltonian cycle*: switching a proper subset of its alternating cycles would give another valid coloring not in the one S3 orbit. Thus an optimum n-edge repair can be carried out by **one** (high-arity) Kempe-cycle switch.

These certificates simultaneously represent an ordered triple of perfect matchings covering each edge once (PM(1)) and, after complementing each matching, a three-sheet cycle double cover (CDC(3)). This is an exact nonlinear bridge to the repository's matching/CDC applications.

**Novelty limitation:** uniqueness under triangle expansions was recorded by Greenwell and Kronk and discussed in https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2016.07.002 and https://doi.org/10.55016/ojs/cdm.v10i2.62320. We claim neither the family nor the group-theoretic mechanism is new.

## A sharp limitation: K3,3 and cube

A **scalar support distance does not determine Kempe reachability**.

On K3,3 the 12 labelled proper edge-colorings are the Latin squares of order 3. They form two S3 orbits, each of six states. Every bichromatic subgraph is a single six-cycle, so a Kempe move acts only as a global color transposition. Consequently the Kempe reconfiguration graph has two components of six states each.

Yet the *unrestricted Hamming-budget-six* reconfiguration graph is connected. Indeed exactly 18 unordered distance-six pairs lie **within** a Kempe component, while exactly 36 unordered distance-six pairs lie **between** components. Therefore two pairs at identical Hamming distance may have opposite Kempe reachability. Increasing the support budget alone cannot merge Kempe components unless the move **language** is enlarged beyond Kempe switches.

The cube Q3 has 24 labelled proper colorings in four S3 color-name orbits, but its Kempe graph is connected. The single-torsor theorem cannot describe the entire cube state graph: Kempe moves connect different global color-action orbits. This is why the existing [component-enriched typed cut relation](component_enriched_three_cut_relation_2026-10-08.md) retains additional fiber indices and move types.

## Research decision

The exact support-Cayley theorem provides a concise common language when certificate states form a single rigid group-action orbit. It **cannot** replace the full response algebra for generic nonlinear systems. The next difficult question is whether small quotient invariants (rather than full internal neutral-component indexes) can nevertheless encode all crossing transitions in broader PM(2)/CDC(q) systems. No such compression theorem has been proved here.
