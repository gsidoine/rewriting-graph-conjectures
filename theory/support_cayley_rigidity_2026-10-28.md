# Support-Cayley rigidity versus Kempe accessibility

**28 October 2026.** Human proofs and independently enumerated finite examples. **No Lean kernel verification; no resolution of an Erdős problem; no literature-priority claim.** The group-action argument, unique 3-edge-colorability and triangle expansions are classical ingredients.

## A general support-Cayley theorem

Suppose a finite group Gamma acts freely and transitively on a finite certificate space S contained in A^I, and the action preserves Hamming distance. Choose a reference certificate s0. Set

    w(g) = number of coordinates on which g*s0 differs from s0;
    T_r = {g ≠ identity : w(g) ≤ r};
    H_r = subgroup generated by T_r.

Under the **unrestricted feasible-to-feasible move rule** allowing any transition changing at most r coordinates, the reconfiguration graph is the Cayley graph Cay(Gamma,T_r). It has exactly [Gamma:H_r] connected components, each of size |H_r|.

**Proof.** Freeness and transitivity identify certificates with group elements. Isometry gives d(g*s0,h*s0) = w(g⁻¹h). Thus a transition occurs exactly when g⁻¹h ∈ T_r, and connected components are the left cosets g H_r. This proof is elementary; the underlying group-action result is not claimed novel.

This extends the view of old-component gauge torsors in [finite-group certificate repair](group_potential_repair_2026-10-28.md) but does **not** assume that the original constraints are group equations. The group action may appear only after solving a nonlinear certificate system.

## Sharp nonlinear example: cubic proper edge-colorings

Let G_k arise from K4 by k successive Y-to-triangle expansions (replace a cubic vertex by a triangle, attaching its three incident edges to the three new vertices). Then n=4+2k and m=3n/2. These are connected, simple, planar, 3-edge-connected cubic graphs.

**Classical rigidity lemma.** K4 has three perfect matchings which are its unique unlabelled 3-edge-coloring; there are six labelled colorings. A Y-to-triangle expansion preserves this count: the three external edge colors of the triangle must be pairwise different, by three-cut parity, and uniquely determine the three internal triangle edge colors. Contracting the triangle gives the inverse correspondence. Thus G_k has precisely six proper labeled 3-edge-colorings, one free transitive S3 orbit under global permutation of color names.

Every color class has n/2 edges. Therefore:
- a color transposition changes exactly n edge labels;
- a 3-cycle of color names changes all 3n/2 edge labels;
- changing the prescribed color of any single edge requires exactly n recolored edges, attained by the corresponding color transposition;
- for move budget r<n the six feasible states are isolated; for n≤r<3n/2 the move graph is K3,3; for r≥3n/2 it is K6;
- any union of two color classes is a *single Hamiltonian cycle*: switching a proper subset of its alternating cycles would give another valid coloring not in the one S3 orbit. Thus an optimum n-edge repair can be carried out by **one** (high-arity) Kempe-cycle switch.

These certificates simultaneously represent an ordered triple of perfect matchings covering each edge once (PM(1)) and, after complementing each matching, a three-sheet cycle double cover (CDC(3)). This is an exact nonlinear bridge to the repository's matching/CDC applications.

**Novelty limitation:** uniqueness under triangle expansions was recorded by Greenwell and Kronk and discussed in https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2016.07.002 and https://doi.org/10.55016/ojs/cdm.v10i2.62320. We claim neither the family nor the group-theoretic mechanism is new.

## A sharp limitation: K3,3 and cube

A **scalar support distance does not determine Kempe reachability**.

On K3,3 the 12 labelled proper edge-colorings are the Latin squares of order 3. They form two S3 orbits, each of six states. Every bichromatic subgraph is a single six-cycle, so a Kempe move acts only as a global color transposition. Consequently the Kempe reconfiguration graph has two components of six states each.

Yet the *unrestricted Hamming-budget-six* reconfiguration graph is connected. Indeed exactly 18 unordered distance-six pairs lie **within** a Kempe component, while exactly 36 unordered distance-six pairs lie **between** components. Therefore two pairs at identical Hamming distance may have opposite Kempe reachability. Increasing the support budget alone cannot merge Kempe components unless the move **language** is enlarged beyond Kempe switches.

The cube Q3 has 24 labelled proper colorings in four S3 color-name orbits, but its Kempe graph is connected. The single-torsor theorem cannot describe the entire cube state graph: Kempe moves connect different global color-action orbits. This is why the existing [component-enriched typed cut relation](component_enriched_three_cut_relation_2026-10-08.md) retains additional fiber indices and move types.

## Research decision

The exact support-Cayley theorem provides a concise common language when certificate states form a single rigid group-action orbit. It **cannot** replace the full response algebra for generic nonlinear systems. The next difficult question is whether small quotient invariants (rather than full internal neutral-component indexes) can nevertheless encode all crossing transitions in broader PM(2)/CDC(q) systems. No such compression theorem has been proved here.


## Stronger theorem: nonabelian holonomy over multiple color-action orbits

The preceding support-Cayley theorem assumes a *transitive* group action. A classical voltage-graph construction removes this assumption.

Let a finite group Γ act **freely** on a finite certificate space S, with an arbitrary symmetric legal-move relation invariant under Γ. Choose representatives s_i of the Γ-orbits. Write each state uniquely as g·s_i. For every legal move from representative s_i to g·s_j, record a directed quotient edge i→j carrying gain g. Equivariance says its lift at group coordinate h is exactly

    (i,h) → (j,h*g).

This is an isomorphism from the original reconfiguration graph to the *derived voltage graph*; it remembers the actual move language, not merely Hamming support.

For each connected component B of the underlying quotient graph, choose a root o and quotient path gains p_i from o to each i∈B (p_o=1). Define

    K_B = subgroup generated by {p_i * g * p_j^(-1)
            : i→j is a quotient edge with gain g, i,j∈B}.

**Exact holonomy theorem.** The full graph above B has precisely

    [Γ : K_B]

connected components, *each* with

    |B| * |K_B|

states. Thus the total number of components is the sum of these indices over the connected quotient components.

**Proof.** Starting at (o,1), closed quotient walks lift to the group labels generated by the gains p_i*g*p_j^(-1). Hence the set of reachable labels at the root is exactly K_B, and the reachable labels above i form K_B*p_i. Every vertex in B therefore contributes |K_B| states to that full component. Starting with a different root label h gives the left coset h*K_B. Distinct left cosets produce distinct components, proving the count and size formula. QED.

This is *classical nonabelian voltage-cover theory* (not a priority claim), but gives an exact, directly executable compression for equivariant graph-certificate systems.

The [verifier](../applications/edge_coloring_voltage.py) constructs this quotient and checks S3-equivariance before computing the holonomy subgroup. The [tests](../tests/test_edge_coloring_voltage.py) compare its predictions with full-state BFS for:

| Certificate/moves | Color-name orbits | Quotient component(s) (|B|,|K_B|) | Full neutral components |
|---|---:|---|---|
| K4 triangle-expansion, budget below n | 1 | (1,1) | 6 singletons |
| Same, budget n / Kempe moves | 1 | (1,6) | one of size 6 |
| K3,3, Kempe moves | 2 | two copies of (1,6) | two of size 6 |
| K3,3, arbitrary six-edge changes | 2 | (2,6) | one of size 12 |
| Cube, Kempe moves | 4 | (4,6) | one of size 24 |

The theorem does **not** automatically extend to action spaces with stabilizers (e.g. repeated matching-coordinate labels in PM(2)); these require a stabilizer-enriched action groupoid or the existing component-enriched typed response formalism.


## General theorem — stabilizer-enriched coset holonomy (nonfree actions)

The voltage theorem above assumes a free action. More generally, let Γ act arbitrarily on a finite certificate space S, preserving a symmetric legal-move relation. Choose an orbit representative s_i for each quotient vertex i and let H_i={g∈Γ:g·s_i=s_i} be its stabilizer. States above i correspond to right-subgroup cosets gH_i.

For each move s_i → d·s_j, record the oriented quotient edge i→j labeled d. Its exact family of lifts is (i,gH_i) → (j,gdH_j); the full set of quotient edges includes the left H_i-translates mandated by equivariance. Gains are naturally defined modulo right H_j. No free-action assumption is needed.

For a connected quotient component B, choose root paths with gains p_i (p_root=1). Define the stabilizer-enriched holonomy subgroup

    K_B = < p_i H_i p_i^(-1) for i∈B,
            p_i d p_j^(-1) for each quotient edge i→j of gain d >.

**Exact coset-holonomy theorem.** The number of global reconfiguration components above B is

    [Γ:K_B],

and every such component has exactly

    sum_{i∈B} |K_B| / |H_i|

states. These quotients are integers because the conjugates p_i H_i p_i^(-1) lie inside K_B.

**Proof.** A state in orbit i is a coset gH_i. Along a lifted walk, the group label changes by right multiplication by the edge gains; inserting a stabilizer at any visited vertex does not change the state. Choosing root paths identifies the group labels accessible above the root with K_B, generated by conjugated stabilizers and edge-cycle gains. Above i, the accessible cosets are K_B p_i H_i / H_i, of cardinality |K_B|/|H_i|. Root labels from distinct left cosets of K_B produce disjoint and isomorphic components. QED.

This is classical finite permutation-group/voltage-groupoid reasoning, not a new literature claim. It reduces to the free voltage formula when all stabilizers are trivial.

### Direct application to PM(2) six-matching covers

On K3,3, encode each of the six perfect matchings as a permutation of three elements. There are exactly **900** ordered six-matching certificates in which every edge is covered twice. The S6 action permuting the six *coordinate positions* is not free because repeated perfect matchings occur.

The 900 certificates form **three S6 orbits** with sizes 90, 720, 90 and stabilizer orders 8, 1, 8. Every pair of different perfect matchings has a symmetric difference consisting of one alternating cycle; a coordinate transposition is therefore one valid binary neutral move. Each orbit is connected by such moves, and the full neutral reconfiguration graph has exactly three components of sizes 90, 720, 90.

The stabilizer-enriched quotient has three isolated orbit nodes, each with K_B=S6 of order 720. Its exact formula gives component sizes 720/8=90, 720/1=720 and 720/8=90, independently agreeing with the pair-rigid tile calculation already archived in the repository.

The [generic stabilizer-aware solver](../applications/edge_coloring_coset.py) and [independent exhaustive verifier](../tests/test_edge_coloring_coset.py) reconstruct these 900 states, check the alternating-cycle property directly, and confirm the exact component sizes against full-state BFS. The tests also check a nonfree S3 action on three constant-color states, both with no moves and with complete connectivity.

**Scope:** An exact small quotient is guaranteed only relative to a given finite action, and can still be exponentially large in the host graph. This does not imply an efficiently bounded response algebra for all PM(2) or CDC(q) graphs. A genuinely new algorithmic theorem would have to exploit extra structure to control that quotient's size.
