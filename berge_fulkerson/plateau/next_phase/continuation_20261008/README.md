# October 8 continuation: quadratic drift and extensive plateaux

**Research branch, not canonical and not externally refereed.** Nothing here resolves Berge–Fulkerson, Fan–Raspaud or the unrestricted two-coordinate descent conjecture.

## Two theorem candidates

**Exact drift on all 3-edge-colourable cubic graphs.** For a six-perfect-matching state with quadratic defect `Q`, on a cubic graph with `n` vertices and a fixed three-factorisation, the sum of the defects of the 45 candidate moves replacing two original coordinates by two *distinct* colour factors is exactly `18Q + 39n`. Thus, if `Q > 13n/9`, some replacement strictly decreases `Q`. Similarly, the sum of 20 triple-coordinate replacement defects is `4Q + 12n`, proving strict three-coordinate descent when `Q > 3n/4`. See `paper/main.tex` for a self-contained proof.

**Linearly large positive plateaux in 3-edge-connected bipartite cubic graphs.** For each `k>=1`, a 3-edge-connected cubic bipartite graph on `16k+2` vertices has six perfect matchings with `Q=6k` and **no strict one-coordinate improvement**. A sequence of `2k` one-coordinate changes has energy trace `[6k,6k,6k-6,6k-6,...,6,6,0]`. This uses a verified boundary-stiff 18-vertex gadget and tight three-edge splicing. The previously published notion of *tight splicing* is not claimed new; the candidate new contribution is its use to amplify these specific quadratic local obstructions.

**Essential novelty correction.** Our *earlier* `cover_lifting.py` construction already inherited one-coordinate local traps under graph coverings, giving unbounded linear defect and density `Q/n=1/3`. The splice construction is therefore **not** the first extensive family. Its extra features are a different 3-edge-cut mechanism, an explicit stiffness inequality, and asymptotic density `3/8`. This reduces the novelty strength of the family in isolation.

**Independent defect-component repair.** For any connected component `C` of the defective edges `m_e != 2` of a *cubic bipartite* sextuple, choose at least two original matching coordinates `I` so that their union covers every overfull edge `e` at least `m_e-2` times. Then there are replacement matchings that correct *exactly* the defects in `C`, preserving the multiplicity of every edge outside `C`. The proof is a regular bipartite multigraph factorization. As a consequence, if all multiplicities lie in `{1,2,3}`, then every state in which all defect cycles have length at most **10** normalizes strictly by a sequence of **two-coordinate** replacements, regardless of total graph order or defect (length at most **38** suffices for three-coordinate replacements). This is a theorem for an infinite class of *starting states*, not a proof of unrestricted two-coordinate descent.

**A witness for the distinction between one-step and iterative repair.** The earlier 32-vertex `Q=34` extremal state needs four coordinates to reach `Q=0` *in one replacement*. Its defect has three connected components and an independently checkable sequence of three **pair** replacements yields the defect trace `[34,24,12,0]`, correcting exactly one component per move. See `componentwise_repair.py`, `q34_three_pair_repair.json`, and `verify_component_certificate.py`.

## Finite investigations

- All **nine** nonisomorphic connected simple cubic bipartite graphs on 6,8,10,12 vertices were generated by exact canonicalisation, yielding **665,112** six-perfect-matching multisets. **None** had a strict two-coordinate local trap at positive Q.
- All **2,324,784** six-matching multiset states of flower snark `J5` were checked in C++ with exhaustive 2-coordinate replacement optimization. Again, no positive-Q trap. This is a finite computation, not a general snark theorem.
- The drift identities were independently checked on all 462 states of K3,3 and 70 sampled states of each of the other eight graph classes (1,022 identities total).
- The base graph has 47 perfect matchings, all enumerated without an external optimizer; its six one-coordinate stiffness conditions were checked against each of the 47 alternatives. Both boundary ports preserve their coordinate choices across the explicit `6 -> 6 -> 0` path.
- Independent checkers validate the first four three-edge splices, including all deletions of zero, one, or two edges and all perfect-matching and defect-trace checks. Weighted matching optimization also checks their one-coordinate local optimality.

## Proof architecture and trust boundary

1. The **drift identity** is an elementary hypergeometric counting proof. Finite tests are checks, not dependencies.
2. The **finite stiffness lemma** is computer-assisted and relies on a tiny explicit base graph (`sharp_18_vertex.json` in the existing plateau directory), exhaustive standard-library perfect-matching enumeration (47 cases), and a transparent quadratic inequality. It is also specified in the manuscript's appendix.
3. The **tight splice lemma** is classical. The new **defect-composition identity** subtracts the cut penalty once, avoiding a tempting but *invalid* argument that local optimality simply glues across a boundary.
4. The infinite family follows by induction and the stiffness inequality, not by extrapolating four tested graphs.
5. Novelty versus existing matching reconfiguration, congestion games and tight-cut literatures has **not** been certified by an external specialist. Lean formalization has not been performed.

## Next research targets

- Prove or disprove strict two-coordinate descent for all cubic bipartite graphs, with no defect restriction. The exhaustive 665,112-state result supports but does not prove it.
- Strengthen the quantitative plateau obstruction without introducing 3-edge cuts; for example, seek linearly high defect local minima among cyclically 4-edge-connected cubic bipartite graphs.
- Formally verify the finite stiffness inequality and the algebraic splice-defect accounting in Lean 4. These are suitable interfaces between computer search and formal mathematics.
