\begin{abstract}
We investigate Collatz-style local normalization systems for Barnette's
Hamiltonicity conjecture, Tutte's 5-flow conjecture, and the
Berge--Fulkerson conjecture.  For Barnette graphs we derive an exact
topological defect formula and exhibit a counterexample to normalization
by one- and two-face flips, while connected three-face flips survive a
large exhaustive test suite.  For integer 6-flows we characterize local
minima of the number of value-5 edges by vertex potentials and polarized
cuts.  For six perfect matchings we prove an unconditional quadratic
descent theorem and a family of conditional multi-replacement descent
bounds.  None of the three classical conjectures is resolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Barnette: an exact defect formula}
Let $G$ be a cubic plane bipartite graph and $H=G^*$ its Eulerian
triangulation dual.  For a nontrivial partition $V(H)=A\sqcup B$, define
\[
 \psi(F)=c(F)-1+\beta(F),\qquad
 \Phi(A,B)=\psi(H[A])+\psi(H[B]).
\]
Let $X$ be the primal subgraph dual to $\delta_H(A)$, let $z$ be the
number of vertices omitted by $X$, and let $t$ be the number of cycle
components of $X$.

\begin{theorem}
\[
 \Phi(A,B)=z+2(t-1).
\]
In particular, $\Phi=0$ if and only if $X$ is a Hamiltonian cycle.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Every triangular face of $H$ has zero or two bichromatic edges, so $X$
is a disjoint union of cycles.  Put $h=|V(H)|$.  Then
$|E(H)|=3h-6$ and $|V(G)|=2h-4$.  Since $X$ is 2-regular on
$2h-4-z$ vertices, $|\delta_H(A)|=2h-4-z$.  Hence the total number of
monochromatic edges is $h-2+z$.  The $t$ disjoint cycles divide the sphere
into $t+1$ monochromatic regions, so $c(H[A])+c(H[B])=t+1$.
Substitution gives the result.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}[Three-face normalization]
Every nontrivial binary colouring of an Eulerian plane triangulation can
be normalized to a two-tree partition by flipping connected patches of
at most three vertices without increasing $\Phi$.
\end{conjecture}

The corresponding assertion for patches of at most two vertices is false;
an explicit 36-vertex Barnette graph has six trapped states.  Exhaustive
tests on 80 generated graphs through 40 vertices cover 139,796,480
colouring states and find no obstruction for patches of size at most three.

\section{Tutte: residual optimality and polarized cuts}
Fix an orientation and an integer 6-flow $f:E\to\{1,\ldots,5\}$.
Give each forward residual arc cost $1$ when $f(e)=4$ and $0$ otherwise,
and each backward residual arc cost $-1$ when $f(e)=5$ and $0$ otherwise.

\begin{theorem}
There is no negative residual cycle if and only if there is an integer
potential $h$ such that, for every oriented edge $x\to y$,
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 f(e)&h(y)-h(x)\\ \hline
 1&\le0\\
 2,3&=0\\
 4&\in\{0,1\}\\
 5&\ge1.
\end{array}
\]
For every threshold $S_k=\{v:h(v)\ge k\}$, if $a$ value-4 edges and
$c$ value-5 edges enter $S_k$, then
\[
 |\delta(S_k)|=5a+6c.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The first assertion is the negative-cycle potential theorem applied to
the residual network.  Threshold crossings have the stated orientations
by the inequalities.  Flow conservation forces $4a+5c$ value-1 edges
to leave, yielding the cut formula.
\end{proof}

\section{Berge--Fulkerson: quadratic descent}
For six perfect matchings let $m(e)$ be the edge multiplicity and put
\[
 Q=\sum_e(m(e)-2)^2.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Unconditional one-replacement descent]
For every bridgeless cubic graph on $n$ vertices, if $Q>2n$, one of the
six current perfect matchings can be replaced by another perfect matching
so as to strictly reduce $Q$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The all-$1/3$ vector belongs to the perfect matching polytope, so there is
a distribution on perfect matchings with marginal $1/3$ on every edge.
Remove one current matching uniformly and add an independent matching from
this distribution.  An edgewise calculation, followed by
$\sum_e(m(e)-2)=0$ and $|E|=3n/2$, gives
\[
 \mathbb E[\Delta Q]=\frac{2n-Q}{3}.
\]
If $Q>2n$, some replacement has negative change.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional $k$-replacement descent]
If a Fulkerson cover exists, then for $1\le k\le5$ there is a
$k$-matching replacement with expected change
\[
 \mathbb E[\Delta Q]
 =\frac{k}{30}\bigl((k-11)Q+4(6-k)n\bigr).
\]
\end{theorem}

This yields strict descent above the respective thresholds
$2n,16n/9,3n/2,8n/7,2n/3$.

\begin{conjecture}[Snark one-replacement normalization]
For every snark, every six-perfect-matching state reaches a Fulkerson
cover through one-matching replacements that never increase $Q$.
\end{conjecture}

The conjecture has been exhaustively verified on all 462 states of the
Petersen graph, all 2,324,784 states of the flower snark $J_5$, and
millions of additional recorded random cubic and bipartite instances.

\section{Conclusion}
The three-face Barnette conjecture and the one-replacement snark
conjecture are stronger normalization forms of their classical parent
problems.  The immediate theorem targets are to orient all low-defect
local minima: topological $\Phi=2$ traps for Barnette, polarized threshold
cuts for 5-flows, and $Q\le2n$ states for Berge--Fulkerson.
